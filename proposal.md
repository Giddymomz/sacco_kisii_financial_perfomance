## CHAPTER ONE: INTRODUCTION

### 1.1 Background of the Study
Savings and Credit Co-operative Societies (SACCOs) are important financial institutions in Kenya. They mobilize savings from members and provide affordable credit, especially to people who cannot easily access commercial banks. In Kisii County, SACCOs support farmers, teachers, boda boda operators, small traders, and civil servants.

However, many SACCOs in Kisii County face poor financial performance, delayed loan repayment, low profitability, and liquidity problems. One major reason is poor financial investment decisions. Managers must decide how much to invest in credit, capital projects, liquid assets, and diversified portfolios. If these decisions are wrong, the SACCO loses money and members lose trust.

Financial investment decisions determine whether a SACCO grows or collapses. This study examines how four investment decisions influence financial performance.

### 1.2 Statement of the Problem
Despite the growth of SACCOs in Kisii County, many report declining Return on Assets (ROA), low Return on Equity (ROE), and high loan default rates. SASRA reports show that some SACCOs struggle to meet withdrawal demands and to pay dividends. The problem is not lack of members or savings, but poor allocation of funds.

There is limited research that combines credit, capital, liquidity, and portfolio decisions in one model for SACCOs in Kisii County. Most past studies focus on one decision only. This study fills that gap.

### 1.3 Purpose of the Study
To establish the influence of financial investment decisions on the financial performance of SACCOs in Kisii County, Kenya.

### 1.4 Objectives of the Study
1. To establish the influence of credit investment decisions on financial performance of SACCOs in Kisii County.
2. To examine the influence of capital investment decisions on financial performance of SACCOs in Kisii County.
3. To determine the influence of liquidity investment decisions on financial performance of SACCOs in Kisii County.
4. To assess the influence of portfolio investment decisions on financial performance of SACCOs in Kisii County.

### 1.5 Research Questions
1. How do credit investment decisions influence financial performance?
2. How do capital investment decisions influence financial performance?
3. How do liquidity investment decisions influence financial performance?
4. How do portfolio investment decisions influence financial performance?

### 1.6 Significance of the Study
- **To SACCO Managers:** Will guide better investment allocation.
- **To SASRA and Regulators:** Will provide evidence for policy on liquidity and capital requirements.
- **To Members:** Better performance means higher dividends and safer savings.
- **To Researchers:** Provides a model that can be replicated in other counties.

### 1.7 Scope and Limitations
The study focuses on licensed SACCOs in Kisii County, Kenya, for the period 2020-2025. It focuses on managers, accountants, and credit officers. Limitation: Some SACCOs may not share full financial data. This will be handled by using anonymized data and secondary data from SASRA reports.

## CHAPTER TWO: LITERATURE REVIEW

### 2.1 Theoretical Framework
**1. Modern Portfolio Theory (Markowitz, 1952):** A SACCO should not put all funds in one investment. Diversification reduces risk and increases return. This supports portfolio investment decisions.

**2. Liquidity Preference Theory (Keynes, 1936):** Institutions must hold enough liquid assets to meet short-term demands. Too much liquidity reduces profit, too little causes collapse. This supports liquidity decisions.

**3. Pecking Order Theory (Myers & Majluf, 1984):** Institutions prefer internal funds, then debt, then equity. This explains capital investment decisions in SACCOs, which rely mainly on member deposits.

### 2.2 Empirical Review
**Credit Investment:** Ochieng (2021) found that strict credit appraisal reduces default and improves ROA in Kenyan SACCOs. Poor appraisal leads to non-performing loans.

**Capital Investment:** Mwangi & Njeri (2022) found that SACCOs that invest in capital assets like buildings and technology improve long-term performance, but over-investment reduces liquidity.

**Liquidity Investment:** Kamau (2023) found a positive relationship between liquidity ratio and financial performance, but excess liquidity lowers profitability.

**Portfolio Investment:** A study by Otieno (2020) in Kisumu found that SACCOs that diversified into government securities and fixed deposits performed better than those that only gave loans.

### 2.3 Conceptual Framework
Independent Variables (X) -> Dependent Variable (Y)

- Credit Investment (loan appraisal, loan recovery) -> Financial Performance (ROA, ROE, Loan Repayment Rate)
- Capital Investment (buildings, technology, branches)
- Liquidity Investment (cash reserves, short-term assets)
- Portfolio Investment (diversification, government securities)

### 2.4 Research Gap
Past studies studied these variables separately and in different counties. No study has combined all four in Kisii County using both primary survey data and secondary financial data. This study fills that gap.

## CHAPTER THREE: RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

### 3.1 Research Design
Descriptive and correlational research design. It describes current investment practices and tests the relationship between investment decisions and performance.

### 3.2 Target Population
All licensed SACCOs in Kisii County (approx. 45 SACCOs). Respondents: SACCO managers, accountants, credit officers, and board members. Target: 135 respondents (3 per SACCO).

### 3.3 Sampling Technique
Stratified random sampling. SACCOs stratified into: Teacher-based, Farmer-based, Employee-based, and Community-based. Sample size determined by Yamane (1967) formula.

### 3.4 Data Collection
- **Primary Data:** Structured questionnaire (see questionnaire.md) using 5-point Likert scale (1=Strongly Disagree to 5=Strongly Agree).
- **Secondary Data:** Audited financial statements and SASRA reports (2020-2025) for ROA, ROE, and loan repayment rates.

### 3.5 Data Analysis
Data will be analyzed using SPSS and Python. Descriptive statistics (mean, standard deviation) and inferential statistics (Multiple Linear Regression) will be used.

**Regression Model:**
ROA = b0 + b1(Credit) + b2(Capital) + b3(Liquidity) + b4(Portfolio) + e

Where:
- b0 = Constant
- b1-b4 = Coefficients
- e = Error term

Significance tested at p < 0.05. Pilot analysis is available in analysis.py and sample_data.csv in this repository.

### 3.6 Ethical Considerations
Permission will be obtained from NACOSTI and Kisii County SACCO management. Respondents will remain anonymous. Data will be used for academic purposes only.
